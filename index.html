    What Muslims believe

    Muslims believe in one God, called in Arabic (the same word Arabic-speaking Christians use for God). They believe God sent prophets to guide people, including Adam, Abraham, Moses and Jesus, and that Muhammad was the last of them.

    Muslims honor Jesus (called ) as a prophet born to Mary, but they do not believe he is God or the Son of God. This is one of the main differences from Christianity.

    The Quran

    The is the book Muslims believe was revealed to the Prophet Muhammad over about 23 years. It is recited in Arabic, and translations are used to understand the meaning. The verse of the day above comes from it.

    How a prayer is done
    1. Wash for prayer (): hands, mouth, nose, face, arms to the elbows, wipe the head and ears, wash the feet.
    2. Stand clean, facing the , and say "Allahu Akbar" (God is the greatest).
    3. Recite the opening chapter of the Quran, Al-Fatiha, standing.
    4. Bow, stand up, then kneel and place your forehead on the ground () twice.
    5. Each set of these movements is a . At the end, sit, recite, and say "peace be upon you" to the right and left.

    Words, number of rak'ahs and posture are best learned by watching someone do it. Ask at a local mosque.

    Want to become Muslim?

    You become Muslim by saying the Shahada sincerely. You do not need to know everything first, and learning continues for life. A good next step is to visit a local mosque, meet the imam, and ask your questions. It helps to learn with real people, not only from a website.

    This guide is a simple introduction, not a full teaching. For deeper questions, ask a trusted Muslim teacher.
